## Repos

- **the-music-constellation/** — the active project. One web app, **four** genre constellations (jazz, hip hop, reggae, **classical**) with a genre switcher. Jazz, hip hop and reggae are hand-authored; classical is **generated by an importer** from `~/Desktop/Composers and Musical Movements/` (712 composers, patrons and theorists; a **local-only git repo**, no remote), the same pattern as the art constellation. Genres are **lazy-loaded**, so a visitor downloads only the one they open. All four offer globe, chord and timeline views. Every artist CV across all four genres is written from a per-artist evidence pack and adversarially fact-checked; the **About the data** tab in Rooms is the public provenance note for that. Full agent context in its `CLAUDE.md`; curation format in `docs/DATA-FORMAT.md`; deferred work in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. Live: https://hueyb-art.github.io/the-music-constellation/
- **the-art-constellation/** — the same engine adapted to **20th-century visual art** (883 artists, 90 movements, 7 eras), as its own standalone project rather than a fourth "genre". Adds a **Timeline view** (movements as dated clusters you pan through time, clusters bloom open on hover) and an **enrichment layer** — biographies, lifespans, portraits and freely-licensed artwork images, harvested from Wikidata/Wikipedia. Source data is authored as markdown in `~/Desktop/20th Century Art Movements/` and imported; that folder is a **local-only git repo** (no remote — this repo is public and the markdown is the curation). See its `CLAUDE.md`. Live: https://hueyb-art.github.io/the-art-constellation/
- **the-science-constellation/** — a third constellation, standalone, with the model **inverted**: the stars are **ideas, not people** (everyone knows what gravity is and cannot name Newton), and the scientist is the reveal when a star opens. 454 concepts from c. 600 BC to the present across 8 eras, 821 idea-to-idea connections of eight kinds, each concept carrying a plain statement of what it claims and, where one exists, its equation typeset with KaTeX. **Source markdown lives in the repo at `source/`** — moved off the Desktop 2026-09-12, because the thing everything is generated from had no history. **Private repo, all rights reserved** — created 2026-09-10, deliberately not on the open licences the earlier repos picked up as scaffolding. Its timeline axis is **piecewise**, not linear, because 2,600 years of ideas are wildly unevenly spread. See its `CLAUDE.md`. Not deployed — runs locally on port 8752.
- **the-surveillance-map/** — a different animal from the constellations: not a curated corpus but a **pipeline with a website on the end of it**. A live world map of surveillance technology aimed at people — deployments, contracts, legislation, abuse, pushback — each dot linked to the reporting. Feeds and public APIs only (18 outlets, 18 Google News queries, GDELT), never HTML scraping; a keyword prefilter discards ~75% for free, then **Claude Haiku** judges what survives and returns a *place name*, never coordinates, so a wrong answer fails against the gazetteer instead of landing a dot in the sea. Canvas, vanilla ES modules, no build step. **Private repo, all rights reserved** — created 2026-09-13, same footing as the science constellation. Needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; refreshed by a GitHub Action four times a day. Not deployed (private repo, so Pages needs a paid plan) — runs locally on port 8763. See its `CLAUDE.md`.
- **the-medical-map/** — directory name only; the map is **Where Science Happens** since 2026-10-11. The second project of the surveillance map's shape, and proof the pattern generalises: a world map of **advances across five fields** — medicine and health, life sciences, physical sciences, computing and engineering, earth and environment. The engine was copied, not shared (two instances is too few to know the common core). Two things make it its own thing: a dot is the **lead research institution**, matched against ROR's 133,640 registered organisations — Huey's explicit choice on 2026-09-13, and the right one because it is *checkable*; and the taxonomy is an **ordered ladder** measuring distance from the world, not a set of categories. There are **two ladders over five fields**: medicine keeps lab → animal → early trial → late trial → approved → in use, the other four share *proposed → demonstrated → tested for real → confirmed → accepted → in use*, named per field. Same six keys, so the palette never changed. Do not merge them — "animal" and "phase 3" carry what "demonstrated" and "confirmed" cannot. It reads **seventeen languages** and registries are primary, news secondary — the opposite of its sibling. Until 2026-10-11 it was a medical map with 114 bolted-on entries, 1,463 to 114; that ratio measured how much pipeline each field had and nothing about the world, so every field now runs the same collectors from `scripts/fields.mjs` and `--limit` is per field. Three gaps between fields are real and must not be papered over: **arXiv collects no affiliations** (3 of 100 physics preprints placeable against 67 for life sciences), so physics and computing have no preprint rung; **no results registry exists outside medicine**; **Europe PMC is a life-science index**. A fifth overlay, the **recognition layer**, sets 2,157 medical laureate-prizes across 28 prizes against the record — still medicine-only, deliberately. **Private repo, all rights reserved.** Needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; the store is still a fixture until it runs. A free `OPENALEX_API_KEY` (no card) lifts the harvest budget tenfold. Not deployed — runs locally on port 8764. See its `CLAUDE.md`.
- **the-cuisine-globe/** — the third map on the surveillance/medical engine, and the first that is an **invitation rather than an instrument**: a globe that celebrates the world's cooking. Hover a country, meet five dishes; click to pin it while you read. A **corpus, not a pipeline** — dishes are written once from harvested Wikipedia evidence and committed, closer in shape to the constellations. The country is the subject rather than a dot on it, so `js/globe.js` gained an **inverse projection** and point-in-polygon hit-testing against the same rings that are drawn. Wikipedia is the source because the obvious alternative, TheMealDB, advertises 195 cuisines and holds recipes for barely a dozen — nothing for Nigeria, Ethiopia, Peru or Bolivia. Its prose is never republished (CC BY-SA); descriptions are written fresh and the article credited. Two editorial positions held deliberately: five dishes are a doorway not a summary, and **contested dishes say so** — the validator warns if nothing anywhere is contested. **Private repo, all rights reserved**, created 2026-09-15. Needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (~$1.50 for the world). Not deployed — runs locally on port 8765. See its `CLAUDE.md`.
- **the-language-map/** — **built 2026-09-24**, and the project with the lowest recurring cost here by construction: one CC BY download from Glottolog, no API key, no model for the base map. A globe of **8,859 languoids across 241 families and 183 isolates**, "Celebrating Global Linguistic Diversity" — Papua New Guinea alone holds 895 languages across 51 families. Four rules decide what appears, each measured rather than assumed: **a languoid appears if it has a place of its own** (filtering to `Level = language` hid Bibbulman, Wajuk, Malyangapa and Yuwaalaraay, but 3,791 of 4,000 sampled dialects sit at exactly their parent's coordinate, so only the 563 with a distinct one are added); **eight imperial languages are excluded by name**, because there is **no usable Indigenous flag anywhere** — Glottolog has no such column and Wikidata's holds 25 languages while excluding Ainu, Māori, Navajo, Warlpiri, Quechua and Sámi; **endangerment is not a proxy for "local"**, since filtering to endangered removes Quechua, Aymara and Kannada; and **community status outranks the grade** — 239 languages Glottolog calls extinct carry a source comment giving a status instead, **66 Awakening and 173 Dormant**, which was in the download all along in a column the build discarded. That last one answered the feasibility note's hardest open question: how the map avoids being a memorial. Search indexes **65,059 alternative names** from AIATSIS, ElCat, MultiTree, Lexvo and WALS, because whose spelling a catalogue makes primary is not a neutral choice — "Noongar" found nothing while Glottolog's primary name is "Nyunga" — and folding transliterates `ŋ`→`ng` so **Yolŋu** and **Yolngu** are one query. A search turns the globe to what it found. Attribution is treated as the CC BY condition it is: the citation is **read from the release at build time** so it cannot drift. **Since 2026-10-07 you can hear it**: Lingua Libre audio for 266 languages (CC BY-SA 4.0 / CC0, speaker credited, whole sentences rather than single words for 108 of them), then **Wikitongues video for 279 more** (2026-10-08) taking "can be heard" to ~450 lit dots, then **sign languages in their own mode** (2026-10-09) — 226 of them in teal, because folding them into "can be heard" is the wrong frame for a language of the hands. The licence rule is **the speaker's, not Commons'**: a clip is used only when both agree it is open. **Private repo, all rights reserved.** Runs locally on port 8766. See its `CLAUDE.md`.
- **nt-companion/** — *working title*, begun as "Eddie bible app" (planning pack written by another AI, archived in `docs/planning-archive/`). The first **native iPhone app** in this workspace: SwiftUI, iOS 18+, no third-party Swift packages, signing team and bundle prefix shared with Mic Assistant. **Claude is the builder; Huey is the tester.** Read any New Testament passage in the **Open English Bible** (CC0) with an explanation through two switchable lenses (**Progressive**, the default, and **Scholarly**): plain English, "Literally, the Greek says…", context, readings with scholars **named and catalogue-checked against Open Library, never quoted**. All 1,876 explanations (938 sections × 2 lenses) are **written in advance by Claude Opus 5.5 through the Batches API (~$84 once) and ship in the app**, so it runs free and offline; only typed questions go live, through one Cloudflare Worker, capped at **$5 a month**. Related teachings come in two groups, *Scripture quoted here* and *Other traditions*, quoted exactly from bundled public-domain texts. **Listen** reads aloud with the spoken word marked (the intended reader finds dense text hard); **Further reading** is a thinker-by-thinker page from bibliographies Huey pastes in. **Two apps from one codebase:** NT Companion ("NT Guide" on the home screen) and **NT Light**, Christianity and Judaism only, with no mention of any other faith anywhere. Both in TestFlight. `docs/DECISIONS.md` (P1–P18) overrides the archive. **Private repo, all rights reserved**, created 2026-10-13. See its `CLAUDE.md`.

## Standing workflow rules (from Huey, 2026-06-12)

1. **Ask first.** Reach alignment via clarifying questions (one at a time, with a recommended answer) before structural changes. Explore the code instead of asking when the code can answer.
2. **Validate, then commit AND push.** Repos must stay current on GitHub, but only push when validation passes (`node scripts/validate.mjs` in the-music-constellation) and the app is stable.
3. **Capture learnings** in the repo's `docs/LEARNINGS.md`; keep READMEs and CLAUDE.md files current as part of the work, not as an afterthought.
4. Commit messages should describe the change (the legacy repos' history is all "Add files via upload" — don't add to it).
